The Retention Loop

The thesis is right: a trader who finishes a session and withdraws is a customer you have to acquire again. But the card is the last brick in that wall, not the first — and Hyperbeat won't sell you theirs.

Can you just integrate Hyperbeat?

Asked and answered — not for the card

Hyperbeat does run a partner programme. Builder Codes give third-party developers fee revenue with attribution, plus the Hyperfolio API for portfolio data. Application, whitelist approval, then build.

What it covers is explicit: vaults (HYPE, USDT, BTC, wVLP, dnHYPE, XAUt), liquid staking via beHYPE minting, and vanilla staking through validator delegation. What it does not cover, anywhere in the public documentation, is Cash, Credit Mode, or the Visa card.

Their card is their moat. Nobody rents out the thing they built the company around.

So Hyperbeat is not a shortcut past Stripe and Bridge. It is something else, and possibly more immediately useful: a yield integration. Which matters here, because yield solves the same problem the card solves, from the other end.

One constraint before anyone gets excited

Hyperbeat's vaults live on HyperEVM. Your users' capital is in a HyperCore perps account, funded through Arbitrum. Routing idle balance into a Hyperbeat vault means another hop across another chain boundary — the same class of friction that makes the card hard. Worth an application to find out the real integration shape, not worth assuming it's free.

The loop you're actually building

Name the leak before choosing the patch

Your framing — don't let them leave after the trading session — is the right one, and it deserves to be stated as a loop with a named gap.

ArriveTelegram link opens a working terminal. No install.
FundOn-ramp or bridge. First real friction, partly solved.
TradeThe session. This part works.
Then whatBalance sits idle earning nothing, or leaves. This is the leak.

↳ if it leaves, you re-acquire. If it sits idle, you earn nothing and neither do they.

ReturnOnly if something pulls them back.

Three things plug that gap, and they are not equally hard:

  • Yield on idle balance. Money that earns is money that stays. Medium effort, needs a partner.
  • Spending. Money you can spend is money you never withdraw. Highest impact, hardest, months out.
  • A reason to open the app tomorrow. Streaks, tiers, quests. Cheapest by a wide margin, and it works on users who have already left.

The card is genuinely the strongest of the three — it converts your product from a place people visit into a place their money lives. But it is gated behind KYC, chain mismatch, settlement float and a geography question you haven't answered. Building it first would mean spending a quarter to plug a leak in a bucket with two users in it.

The constraint nobody in this roadmap fixes

Read this before the sequence

You have two users. No item below changes that.

Every plan in these documents makes the product better for people who are already here. Tiers, streaks, rebates, cards, HIP-3 markets — all retention and depth instruments, and retention of two people is not a business.

Your advantage is distribution, and it is real: a Telegram link opens a working perp terminal with no app store between the user and the trade, in Russian, with referrals that ride the messenger's own graph. But an advantage in distribution is not distribution. Somebody has to go and use it.

So treat the roadmap below as the thing that makes acquisition worth doing — a program worth farming, a reason to stay past the first session — and treat acquisition itself as the parallel track that decides whether any of it matters. If you only do one thing this month, make it the channel, not the code.

Last · the bond is the small part, the operational responsibility is the large one

Correction on local currency

Trading a local currency does not mean holding or settling in one. Permissionless quote assets on HyperCore must satisfy strict dollar-peg requirements, so local-currency collateral is not a usable wedge.

The viable form is a USDC-settled perpetual tracking a rate — BRL/USD, TRY/USD — exactly as the existing EUR, JPY and KRW markets work. The user's hedge is the position; the collateral stays dollar-denominated throughout.

Local equities should likewise begin as a regional index or curated basket. Single names drag in exchange hours, holidays, dividends, splits, halts, licensed market data and jurisdictional exposure — an index absorbs most of that.

What would tell you this is working

Pick the measures before you build, or you'll rationalise afterwards

Trade every market from Telegram. Build your rank. Get real benefits back.

That is the promise the roadmap is building toward, and it is worth writing down now because it disciplines the sequence: every market is the HIP-3 front-end work, build your rank is the program, real benefits is Credits funded from revenue you actually collect. Nothing on this page that fails to serve one of those three is worth doing yet.

  • Balance retained 7 days after a session. The direct measure of the leak. Everything in horizons one and two should move it.
  • Return rate on day 2 and day 7. What streaks and tiers are actually for.
  • Referrals per active user, split by rung. If dry invites spike and funded ones don't, you're being farmed and the multiplier design is doing its job by making it worthless.
  • Second card funding. The single question the refer-out experiment exists to answer. One funding event is curiosity; two is a habit.
  • Reconciliation drift staying at zero. You now have the tooling. A program used for allocation later needs a clean record from the start.
